Quick rules reminder

How a roll works

You roll one hundred. Rolling equal to or under your skill is a success. The app tells you which level you reached.

  • Critical: you rolled 1.
  • Extreme success: one fifth of your skill or less.
  • Hard success: half your skill or less.
  • Regular success: your skill or less.
  • Fail: over your skill.
  • Fumble: 100. Also 96 or more if your skill is under 50.

Pushing a roll

If you fail, you may try once more, but you must say what your investigator does differently. If the pushed roll also fails, the Keeper inflicts a real consequence. You cannot push combat rolls or Sanity rolls.

Bonus and penalty dice

The Keeper may grant a bonus die when conditions favour you, or a penalty die when they work against you. The app rolls the extra dice and takes the right one for you. One bonus and one penalty cancel out.

Wounds

  • First Aid heals 1 hit point. Medicine heals 1D3.
  • A major wound is losing half your maximum hit points or more from one attack.
  • At 0 hit points without a major wound you are unconscious.
  • At 0 hit points with a major wound you are dying.
  • Damage equal to your maximum hit points in one blow kills you outright.

Sanity

A loss written as one slash one D eight means: roll under your current Sanity and lose 1, roll over and lose 1D8. Losing 5 or more at once means you roll against INT; passing that roll means you understood what you saw and go temporarily insane for 1D10 hours.

Pushing

  • Only after a failure, and only if the player says what they are doing differently.
  • One push per roll. A failed push means a real consequence, not just failure.
  • Never on combat rolls or Sanity rolls.

Bonus and penalty dice

  • Roll an extra tens die. Bonus keeps the lower total, penalty keeps the higher.
  • One bonus and one penalty cancel out.
  • Point-blank firearms, outnumbering, and a held-up trash can lid all grant one.

Opposed rolls

  • Extreme beats Hard beats Regular beats Fail.
  • Draw: higher skill value wins. Still tied: both roll 1D100, lower wins.
  • Grabbing the floating knife is the exception — on a draw, whoever's turn it is wins.

A combat round

  1. DEX order, highest first. Gun already drawn: add 50 to that investigator's DEX.
  2. Attacker rolls Fighting or Firearms.
  3. Defender chooses Dodge (needs to equal or beat the attacker) or fight back with Fighting (needs to beat them outright, and deals damage if it works).
  4. Fighting back can only ever do regular damage. Only the initiator can do extreme damage.
  5. Extreme damage: blunt is max damage + bonus; impaling is max + another damage roll + bonus.
  6. Once a character has already responded this round, further melee attacks on them get a bonus die.

Wounds

  • Major wound: half maximum hit points or more, from one hit.
  • 0 HP, no major wound: unconscious. 0 HP with one: dying.
  • Damage equal to maximum HP in one blow: dead immediately.
  • First Aid heals 1. Medicine heals 1D3.
  • Healing: 1 HP per day. With a major wound, a weekly CON roll for 1D3, or 2D3 on an Extreme.

Sanity

  • Written X/Y: roll under current Sanity to lose X, over to lose Y.
  • A failed roll lets you dictate one involuntary action.
  • Losing 5 or more at once: roll against INT. Passing means temporary insanity for 1D10 hours.
  • Temporary insanity: roll 1D10 for a bout of madness, lasting 1D10 rounds.

The rules, condensed

The whole system in six lines

  1. Call for a roll only when the outcome is uncertain and dramatic.
  2. Agree the goal first. Success means the goal is achieved.
  3. Roll 1D100. Equal to or under the skill is a Regular success; under half is Hard; under one fifth is Extreme.
  4. 1 is a critical. 100 is a fumble, as is 96+ when the skill is under 50.
  5. A failed roll can be pushed once, if the player justifies it. A failed push costs them something real.
  6. On a success, the player ticks that skill for possible improvement later.

Difficulty

You set difficulty by declaring which level is needed before the roll: Regular for ordinary tasks, Hard for difficult ones, Extreme for the limits of human capability. A useful yardstick from the text: if the task needs a professional level of skill, ask for Hard.

Opposed rolls

Both sides roll. Extreme beats Hard beats Regular beats Fail. On a draw, the higher skill value wins; if those are also equal, both roll 1D100 and the lower wins.

Bonus and penalty dice

Roll an extra tens die. For a bonus, keep the better (lower) result; for a penalty, keep the worse (higher). One of each cancels out. Mostly used for opposed rolls.

Luck

Use Luck when the question is about circumstance rather than ability: is there a loose plank to hand, does the torch still work, is a cab passing. For a group Luck roll, the lowest Luck present rolls for everyone.

Sanity

  • Loss is written X/Y. Roll equal to or under current Sanity to lose X; over to lose Y.
  • Failing a Sanity roll lets you dictate one involuntary action: a scream, a freeze, a dropped weapon.
  • Losing 5 or more at once means major emotional trauma. The player rolls 1D100 against INT. Passing means they comprehended it and go temporarily insane for 1D10 hours; failing means their mind closed to it and they stay sane.
  • Temporary insanity means a bout of madness, and you may add a phobia or mania, or corrupt a backstory entry.
  • During insanity you may present delusions. A player can ask for a "reality check" — a Sanity roll — to see through one. Failing sinks them deeper.

Combat sequence

  1. Everyone acts in DEX order, highest first. A gun already drawn adds 50 to that investigator's DEX.
  2. A round is "long enough for everyone to take one significant action".
  3. An attacker rolls Fighting or Firearms. The defender chooses to dodge (Dodge skill: they need to equal or beat the attacker's level) or fight back (Fighting skill: they need to beat it).
  4. The winner takes no damage and deals damage. Fighting back can only ever achieve regular damage; only the initiator can deal extreme damage.
  5. Extreme damage from a blunt weapon is maximum damage plus damage bonus. From an impaling weapon it is maximum damage, plus another damage roll, plus damage bonus.
  6. Combat rolls cannot be pushed.

Fighting maneuvers

Disarm, knock down, or seize. Treated as a normal Fighting attack. Compare Build: the initiator takes one penalty die per point of Build deficit, to a maximum of two. If the opponent's Build exceeds theirs by three or more, the maneuver simply cannot work.

Outnumbered

Once a character has already responded to an attack this round, every further melee attack on them that round gets one bonus die. Firearms do not benefit.

Wounds and healing

  • First Aid heals 1 hit point; Medicine heals 1D3.
  • Major wound: losing half or more of maximum hit points from a single blow.
  • 0 hit points without a major wound: unconscious. With one: dying.
  • Damage equal to or greater than maximum hit points in one blow: instant death.
  • Healing without a major wound: 1 hit point per day.
  • With a major wound: a CON roll at the end of each week. Success heals 1D3, an Extreme success heals 2D3. The major wound clears on an Extreme success, or once hit points reach half maximum.

The Haunting: scene by scene

Opening

Each player takes one investigator and names them. The premise: they are hired to investigate an old house in 1920s Boston that may be haunted. Read Handout 1.

Mr. Steven Knott recently inherited the property. He wants to rent or sell it, and cannot, because of its reputation. He pays $20 a day and hands over the keys, the address, and $20 in advance.

Players will often want to go straight to the house. Steer them toward research first.

Location 2 — The Boston Globe

Mention the "morgue", the clippings files in the basement. Access is gated by Arty Wilmot, a pompous editor who starts out unhelpful. Goal: gain access to the clippings files.

  • Friendly approach: Charm. Aggressive: Intimidate. Reasoned: Persuade. Conning him: Fast Talk.
  • Regular difficulty — Arty is not a professional-grade social operator.
  • Success: he backs down. Failure: he puffs up and throws them out.
  • They may push by pressing him, and may switch approach. A failed push must escalate beyond "leave" — he calls maintenance men, or it comes to blows.

Inside, give Handout 2 freely — they already paid for it with the social roll. Ruth Blake, the records keeper, will mention the files go back no further than an 1878 fire.

Location 3 — The Central Library

One Library Use roll per half-day of research, per player. No pushing needed: they can simply keep trying, but each attempt costs another half-day. Each success yields the next handout in order: 3, 4, 5, 6. After more than a day, have Knott chase them for progress.

Location 4 — The Hall of Records

Library Use to earn Handout 7, same half-day rule. If they look up the Chapel of Contemplation, the 1912 records are referenced but missing. A successful Law roll, or simple courtesy toward the clerk, points them to the Central Police Station.

Location 5 — Higher Courts and Central Police Station

Needs one successful roll for Handout 8. Any of:

  • Law — they know Kim Debrun, a court clerk. Roll concealed and do not reveal the result. On a success Kim is warm and helpful; on a failure Kim demands a bribe, then reneges and denies knowing them.
  • Credit Rating — works on a success only if their Credit Rating is 75 or more.
  • Persuade, Charm (a flutter of eyelids), or Fast Talk (a fake ID — risky here).

A failed push with Credit Rating, Persuade or Charm earns police antipathy: a shakedown or threats.

Location 6 — The Neighborhood

The Corbitt House is now the only private residence on the block. Mr. Dooley, a cigar and newspaper vendor, knows the area. Roll 1D100 against the investigator's APP or Credit Rating for his reaction; on a poor reaction another investigator may try Charm, Fast Talk, Persuade or Intimidate.

What Dooley gives up:

  • The Macario family moved in a couple of years ago.
  • A year later the father had a serious accident, then went violently mad.
  • He babbled about a haunting form with burning eyes.
  • About a month ago Mrs. Macario went mad too.
  • Both are at Roxbury Sanitarium, a few miles out.
  • The children went to relatives in Baltimore.

He can also point out where the Chapel of Contemplation stood, a few blocks away, and calls the house "the Corbitt House".

Location 7 — Roxbury Sanitarium

Vittorio Macario is quite mad, clutching a bible. At some point he opens it at random, points, and "quotes": "By his own weapon is the devil worsted!" It is not a real verse. It is the clue that Corbitt can be killed with his own dagger. Do not oversell it.

Gabriela Macario is lucid. An evil presence lives in the house. She would wake to find it leaning over her. When angry it threw dishes and objects around the room. Above all it hated Vittorio. End the interview quickly — the questions distress her.

The two boys in Baltimore know nothing except that they miss their parents and had nightmares of a strange man with burning eyes.

Location 8 — The Chapel of Contemplation

Ruins at the end of a crooked street. On a slumping wall, freshly painted in white: three Y shapes arranged in a triangle, their tops touching, with a staring eye at the centre. That is Handout 9.

Near the symbol, describe a tingling in their foreheads, like a headache but not quite. It stops when they leave.

  • As they prowl the rubble, the earth is covering weakened floorboards. Call for Luck rolls.
  • Anyone who fails Luck must make a Jump roll to reach safety.
  • Failing Jump means a ten-foot fall into the basement for 1D6 hit points. They may push Jump with a last-minute grab for the edge; a failed push might break a treasured possession rather than simply hurting more.

In the sealed basement: two skeletons in tattered silk robes beside a cabinet, holding mouldering church records. Under the cabinet, on a Spot Hidden roll (pushable with a thorough search):

  • A journal recording that Walter Corbitt was buried in the basement of his house, "in accordance with his wishes and with the wishes of that one who waits in the dark".
  • A huge worm-eaten Latin volume: the Liber Ivonis. Reading it needs Language (Other) Latin at 50%+ or a successful roll, and three hours. It grants +2 Cthulhu Mythos and drops maximum Sanity from 99 to 97.

Location 9 — The Old Corbitt Place

Brick, overshadowed by taller office buildings, set back in shadow with blank curtained windows. The front door has one lock plus four bolts added in the last year or two. Every ground-floor window is nailed shut from the inside.

Ground floor

  • 1 — Storage. Boxes and junk; a cupboard boarded shut. Forced open, it holds three Corbitt diaries in English: two days to read, +4 Cthulhu Mythos, lose 1D4 Sanity. They describe Corbitt's experiments and the spell "Call Forth the Opener of Ways" (Summon/Bind Dimensional Shambler), which takes 2D6 weeks to learn and is not meant to be used in this scenario.
  • 2 — Second storage. Old furniture, fit for firewood.
  • 3 — Mud room. Coats, galoshes, umbrellas, coal. The side door has three bolts and two locks.
  • 4 — Living room. Radio, couch, shelves — and an unusual quantity of crosses, images of the Virgin, and other Catholic artifacts.
  • 5 — Dining room. Mahogany table, seven chairs, three places set and unused, rice soup rotting in a tureen.
  • 6 — Kitchen. Icebox, wood stove, meagre larder, rat spoor. The fuse box is here.

Build the creep steadily. Corbitt haunts the place: at any moment a noise may come from the spare bedroom upstairs.

Upper floor

  • 1 — Main bedroom. Vittorio and Gabriela's. More crosses, many candles, a rosary and breviary by the bed.
  • 2 — Children's bedroom. Two small beds, toys, pictures of aircraft and cowboys.
  • 3 — Spare bedroom. Bed frame, bare springs, a dresser. This was Corbitt's own room. His influence lingers and he can will things to happen here. Whenever he does, a horrible smell manifests — a sure sign of the Mythos.

What Corbitt can do in that room: loud thumping audible anywhere in the house; pools of blood forming on the floor or dripping from the ceiling; rattling and scratching at the windowpane or door; and the bed attack.

The bed attack. He lures them to inspect the window, then hurls the bed across the room. Ask the investigator at the window for Spot Hidden; on a success they may Dodge. A hit throws them through the window for 1D6+2 hit points. Anyone who sees the bed move makes a Sanity roll at 1/1D4. Pick the target impartially — lowest Luck, or roll at random among those by the window. This can deliver a major wound; if an investigator dies, bring in Mr. Knott the landlord for that player.

The basement

The door has a lock and three bolts, openable only from the upstairs side. Corbitt has cut the power at the kitchen fuse box, and can cut it again whenever it suits him. The walls are lined with close-fitted boards; a cursory inspection reveals hollow areas behind them.

The stairs are in poor repair, and Corbitt can make them move. Each investigator descending makes a combined DEX or Climb roll — one roll, compared against both values, either one succeeding. On a failure, tell them going further risks injury: they can stop, or push and risk a fall of 1D6 hit points.

If one investigator who made it offers to help another, grant the second a bonus die — but on a failure, both fall and take damage. Shared risk, better odds, real tension.

  • 1 — Storage. Tools, pipe, a trash can lid, lumber, nails. A Spot Hidden here is an obscure clue: on a success they find the floating knife and may pick it up, whereupon it fights to break free. A failed pushed Spot Hidden means a hand catches the blade for an automatic 1D4+2 — no attack roll needed.
  • 2 — Empty coal bin. The outside chute is nailed shut.
  • 3 — Corbitt's hidden lair. Behind the boards, a crawl space. Carved into the inner wall: "Chapel of Contemplation", in irregular scratchy letters. Treat as an obvious clue; do not ask for a roll.
  • 4 — Corbitt's hiding place. See the Corbitt screen.

Endings

  • If Corbitt is destroyed, each participating investigator gains 1D6 Sanity.
  • They may claim the worm-eaten book.
  • Knott pays their fee plus a $30 bonus.
  • If it goes badly: the dead may be reanimated to repair the basement walls before crumbling to dust; the insane flee screaming into the night.
  • If they win, you may ignore the lingering effects of Corbitt's claw attacks.
  • Destroying the house without confronting Corbitt is a valid ending — they may be arrested for arson and have to prove their innocence.

Walter Corbitt and the knife

The floating knife

An old knife with an ornate hilt, its blade coated in thick rust that is dried blood. If they find it, Corbitt attacks with it. If they do not, he uses it the moment they start tearing down the wall that hides his body.

  • Costs Corbitt 1 Magic point per round. One attack per round.
  • Roll 1D100 against Corbitt's POW 90; the investigator rolls Dodge. Compare levels.
  • Corbitt fails on 91+. Regular is 46–90, Hard is 19–45, Extreme is 18 or below.
  • A hit deals 1D4+2. An Extreme success impales for 6+1D4+2.
  • If the investigator has not noticed the knife, give the attack a bonus die, allow no Dodge, and let it miss only on a fumble of 100. A Spot Hidden roll is what notices it rising.
  • Seeing the knife attack: Sanity roll at 1/1D4.
  • A garbage can lid held up for protection grants a bonus die on Dodge.
  • Holding the knife: Corbitt may wrench it back each round at the cost of another Magic point. The player keeps hold by winning an opposed roll of their STR against Corbitt's POW.

When the knife rises, start combat rounds. Corbitt's DEX is low, so investigators usually act first. If they flee, it attacks whoever is last to the stairs.

Corbitt himself

Lying motionless on a pallet: a drawn, wooden-looking, wizened figure of some six feet. Skinny and naked, wide-flaring saucer-like eyes, a nose like a knife blade, a chain about his neck. Hairless, shrunken gums making the teeth look very long. A sharp sweet churning scent, like rotten corn.

Spending 2 Magic points lets him move for five combat rounds. He is reluctant to move at all unless threatened.

When he rises: Sanity roll 1/1D8. Anyone who fails makes one involuntary action of your choosing. Anyone losing 5 or more rolls against INT; passing means temporary insanity.

Statistics

Walter Corbitt, Undead Fiend. STR 90, CON 115, SIZ 55, INT 80, POW 90, DEX 35, APP 05, EDU 80. SAN 0 — he ignores the Sanity cost of his spells.

  • Hit points 16. At 0 he crumbles to dust.
  • Magic points 18, recovering 1 per hour. Track these; the scenario turns on them.
  • Damage bonus +1D4. Build 1. Move 8.
  • Fighting 50% (Hard 25, Extreme 10), damage 1D3 + 1D4 damage bonus, plus possible infection.
  • Dodge 17% (Hard 8, Extreme 3).
  • DEX 35. POW 90.
  • Skills: Cthulhu Mythos 17, Intimidate 64, Listen 60, Sleight of Hand 30, Stealth 72. Improvise any others.
  • Armor: Flesh Ward is already cast. Roll 2D6 for its value. Each point of damage reduces it by one.
  • Attacks per round: 1. Spending a Magic point to send the dagger counts as his action for that round.

Claw infection

If he lands a successful attack, the investigator makes a Luck roll. On a failure they are raked by his fingernails: a day later they become delirious and must make a CON roll.

  • Failure: delirium for 1D10 days, and lose 1D10 CON.
  • Success: delirium for 1D6 days, no further loss.
  • Repeat until they recover or die. Lost CON never returns.

Spells

Dominate (Corbitt's variant) — 1 Magic point, one round to cast. Works only on a target inside the house. Opposed POW roll against his POW 90. On his success the victim is dazed for 1D6+1 rounds and obeys telepathic commands: hallucinations, or reckless, idiotic or homicidal acts, though never suicidal ones. They remember nothing afterward.

Flesh Ward — variable Magic points, five rounds to cast, already in effect when they arrive. Each point spent gives 1D6 armor against non-magical attacks. It wears off as it blocks damage and cannot be reinforced or recast until used up. Lasts 24 hours.

Summon/Bind Dimensional Shambler — listed, not used here.

Combat order

  • List investigators and Corbitt by DEX, highest first. A gun already drawn adds 50 to that investigator's DEX.
  • Corbitt acts on DEX 35, or DEX+50 in a round where he casts Dominate.
  • Attacked with Fighting, he fights back with his Fighting skill — including against later attacks in the same round.
  • Attacked with Firearms, just roll to hit; he never dives for cover. Point-blank range grants a bonus die.
  • The second investigator to attack him gets a bonus die for outnumbering.

Playing him

  • He is full of malice and will try to divide the investigators and turn them against each other.
  • He seeks perverse amusement at their expense.
  • He wants any Mythos knowledge they carry into the house — the Liber Ivonis especially.
  • He does not breathe. Have him growl, screech, cackle or mock rather than speak plainly.

Reality checks

If a player questions a delusion, offer a reality check: a Sanity roll. Success drops the delusions and you present no more. Failure costs 1 Sanity, triggers another bout of madness, and you escalate. Because the insanity here comes from a Mythos manifestation, add 5% Cthulhu Mythos to that investigator.

Killing him

  • Reaching 0 hit points, he crumbles into dust and never returns.
  • If they wrest his own dagger away and stab him with it, he turns to ash at once, regardless of any spell.
  • He never heals and cannot be knocked unconscious. He does not breathe. Sunlight pains him and may or may not kill him — your call.
  • Track his Magic points carefully. He recovers 1 per hour. He casts Flesh Ward as soon as anyone enters, so investigators who run straight for his body may catch him before he has regained those two points.

Bouts of madness

Choose one, or roll 1D10. Each lasts 1D10 rounds.

Not rolled

Tap the button above, or read the list.

  1. Amnesia. No memory of anything since they were last somewhere safe. One moment breakfast, the next a monster.
  2. Psychosomatic disability. Blindness, deafness, or loss of the use of a limb.
  3. Violence. A red mist; uncontrolled violence against surroundings, allies and foes alike.
  4. Paranoia. Everyone is out to get them. No one can be trusted. They are being spied on.
  5. Significant person. They mistake someone in the scene for a person from their backstory, and act on that relationship.
  6. Faint. They pass out.
  7. Flee in panic. Compelled to get as far away as possible by any means, even taking the only vehicle.
  8. Physical hysterics or emotional outburst. Incapacitated: laughing, crying, screaming.
  9. Phobia. A new phobia, such as fear of confined spaces, of spirits, or of cockroaches. They imagine the source is present even when it is not.
  10. Mania. A new mania — compulsive washing, compulsive lying, an excessive liking for worms — which they seek to indulge.

In this scenario

Corbitt rising is realistically the only thing that can cause insanity here, so the scope for delusions is limited. Insanity lasts 1D10 hours, or until they leave the house and get a good night's rest. Add a fitting entry to their backstory. One suggested delusion: a fleeing investigator finds a photograph that seems to show Corbitt with their own grandfather, naming them as kin.

Running it for a blind player

Four habits that do most of the work

  • Say the target number before anyone rolls. "Give me a Spot Hidden, Regular." It costs nothing, keeps you honest, and means she never has to ask.
  • Describe rooms in a fixed order. Pick one — exits, then furniture, then detail — and never vary it. Predictable structure is navigable structure.
  • Name the person before the question. "Martha, what are you doing?" not "What are you doing?" She always knows when a question is hers.
  • Say who is speaking when you switch NPCs. Sighted players read it off your face; she cannot.

Things in this scenario that need care

  • Handout 9 is a picture. It is on the Handouts screen as a description she can read. Do not just slide a page across the table.
  • The house map. Describing the layout room by room as they move works better than any map. The room lists on the runsheet screen are already in walking order.
  • The bed attack depends on who is standing where. State positions out loud before you spring it, so the choice feels fair rather than arbitrary.
  • Combat positions. Say where everyone is at the top of each round. Two sentences, every round.

Before session one

Run a twenty-minute taster: one skill roll, one pushed roll, one Sanity roll, one combat round. That is genuinely the whole game, and it gets everyone used to the app and to the table conventions before anything is at stake.
